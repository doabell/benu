# Middlebury dining halls: architectural silhouette research

## Purpose

This note identifies the visible architectural cues that can distinguish Middlebury College's Proctor, Ross, and Atwater dining halls in small illustrations or silhouette marks. The priority is the buildings' actual massing, rooflines, materials, and glazing—not generic dining imagery or loosely related campus architecture.

## Proctor Dining Hall / Proctor Student Center

The correct architectural reference is Freeman French Freeman's **Proctor Student Center at Middlebury College**. The firm's project page documents a 44,000-square-foot renovation completed in 2009 and says the kitchen, servery, and dining hall were redesigned as part of the work ([Freeman French Freeman](https://www.fffinc.com/project/proctor-hall/)).

### Concrete visual cues

- **Broad stone facade:** The exterior photographs show a long, low facade dominated by irregular local-stone wall surfaces.
- **Multiple front-facing gables:** Several pitched roof sections terminate as a repeated series of gabled faces. This multi-gable rhythm is Proctor's clearest skyline cue.
- **Low, spread-out massing:** Unlike Ross's residential tower or Atwater's single oval pavilion, Proctor reads as a horizontally extended building assembled from connected pitched-roof volumes.
- **Recessed openings beneath the roofs:** Windows and entrances sit within the heavier stone composition; glazing is secondary to the roof and masonry silhouette.

**Silhouette direction:** Use a long stone base capped by three or more unequal, front-facing gables. Preserve the stepped multi-gable roofline before adding window or door detail. A plain rectangular pavilion would miss the building's most recognizable feature.

### Source warning

[Maclay Architects' “Proctor Academy Dining Commons”](https://www.maclayarchitects.com/portfolio-project-10-proctordh.php) is explicitly located in **Andover, New Hampshire** and belongs to **Proctor Academy**. It is not Middlebury College's Proctor Hall and should not be used as evidence for the Benu depiction.

## Ross Dining Hall / Ross Commons and LaForce Hall

TSKP Studio presents Ross as a combined residential-and-dining complex at Middlebury. Its project description identifies granite stone cladding and tinted, triple-glazed curtain walls, while its exterior images show the defining relationship between the tall residence block and the low dining wing ([TSKP Studio](https://tskp.com/work/project_page/ross_commons_dining_laforce_residential_hall)).

### Concrete visual cues

- **Tall stone residential block:** LaForce Hall rises as the dominant, multi-story masonry mass, with a pitched/curving roof profile and repeated punched windows.
- **Long, low dining wing:** The dining portion stretches outward at the base as a much lower horizontal volume.
- **Curved descending roof:** The low wing's roof sweeps and steps downward with the slope rather than forming a conventional gable. Middlebury's architectural history likewise describes descending curved dining-hall roofs that follow the falling hillside ([Middlebury, North Campus](https://sites.middlebury.edu/walkingmiddlebury/north-campus/)).
- **Broad glazed edge:** Large curtain-wall areas make the dining wing read as lighter and more transparent than the stone residential block. TSKP states that the design uses tinted triple-glazed curtain-wall systems and preserves mountain view corridors.

**Silhouette direction:** Compose Ross as a strong vertical stone block joined to a long, low, curved-roof and glazed wing. Both masses matter: using only the roof arcs loses the residential landmark, while using only the tall block loses the dining hall's distinctive sweep and transparency.

## Atwater Dining Hall / Atwater Commons

Atwater is a freestanding **elliptical or oval dining pavilion**, visually defined by a planted roof that slopes over a curved glazed perimeter. Sika's case study describes the sloping green roof as continuing the landscape up and over the building, and its project photographs show the pavilion's curved edge and low landscape-integrated profile ([Sika Sarnafil](https://usa.sika.com/sarnafil/en/sika-at-work/food-beverage/atwater-dining-hall.html)). Middlebury independently calls the building oval-shaped, largely window-walled, and topped by a vegetated roof ([Middlebury opening announcement](https://www.middlebury.edu/announcements/2005/02/new-environmentally-friendly-dining-hall-include-vegetated-roof)).

### Concrete visual cues

- **Elliptical pavilion:** The plan and outer wall read as one continuous rounded form rather than a rectilinear hall.
- **Sloping planted roof:** The roof rises asymmetrically and carries native grasses and shrubs. Its planted incline is the primary silhouette feature, not a decorative accent.
- **Curved glazing:** A broad curved window wall follows the pavilion's perimeter beneath the roof edge, producing a light, transparent base under the heavier planted cap.
- **Building-as-landscape:** The roof visually extends the surrounding slope over the structure; the pavilion appears settled into the hillside rather than placed on top of it.

**Silhouette direction:** Use a low elliptical footprint, one clearly sloping planted roof plane, and a curved glazed band beneath it. Avoid turning the building into a generic flat oval or domed rotunda—the incline and landscape continuity are essential.

KieranTimberlake designed Atwater Commons. Its supplied project URL was unavailable during this research pass, but it should remain in the source record; KieranTimberlake's archived project listings identify Atwater Commons at Middlebury College, while Sika and Middlebury corroborate the pavilion's form ([KieranTimberlake](https://kierantimberlake.com/work/atwater-commons/)).

## Comparative design recommendation

The three buildings can form a coherent family if they share line weight, baseline, and level of detail while retaining different massing signatures:

| Hall    | Primary silhouette                                     | Secondary cue              |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Proctor | Long stone facade with repeated front-facing gables    | Recessed openings          |
| Ross    | Tall stone residential block plus long low curved wing | Broad curtain-wall glazing |
| Atwater | Elliptical pavilion under a sloping planted roof       | Curved glazed perimeter    |

At small sizes, preserve those massing contrasts before texture: **Proctor = multi-gable**, **Ross = tower plus sweep**, **Atwater = planted ellipse**.
